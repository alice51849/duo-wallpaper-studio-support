How to read the results

  • Cover and inner wallpaper: export each image at exactly your screen's pixel size. An image that matches the screen is shown as is; anything else gets scaled or cropped by the phone, and that's where heads get cut off and edges go soft.
  • Aspect ratio: if you'd rather crop in a photo editor than type pixels, lock the crop to this ratio, then resize.
  • One source picture for both: the inner screen shows the whole picture, and the cover shows a close-up cut from it. The source has to be big enough that the close-up still has as many pixels as the cover screen, otherwise the cover wallpaper is upscaled and looks blurry. A tighter close-up needs a bigger source.
  • Cover crop: the size of the box to cut from the source picture for the cover, then shrink to the cover size above.

Making one scene look good on both screens

  1. Start wide. The inner screen is where the whole scene lives, so pick or paint a picture with room around the subject. A photo already cropped tight to a face leaves nothing to reveal when the phone opens.
  2. Give the cover one clear subject. The outside screen is small, and the clock and notifications sit on top of it. A person, a doorway, a single tree or the moon reads well there. A busy crowd doesn't.
  3. Keep the subject off the fold. On a book-style phone the hinge runs down the middle of the inner screen. Put faces and text a little to one side so they don't sit on the crease.
  4. Leave room for the clock. Keep the top of the cover crop calm (sky, wall, soft color) so the time stays easy to read.
  5. Use the same colors on both. When the cover crop really is a piece of the inner picture, closing and opening the phone feels like zooming in and out of one design instead of switching between two wallpapers.
  6. Check it on the phone. Some home screens move or crop the wallpaper a little. Set both images, look at them closed and open, and nudge the crop if something important is cut off.

Where to find your screen resolution

Your phone's spec sheet lists the resolution of each screen. On many Android phones you can also find it in Settings, under the display section or About phone. Type the numbers the way you hold the phone: for most cover screens that's narrower than tall, and for book-style inner screens it's close to square.

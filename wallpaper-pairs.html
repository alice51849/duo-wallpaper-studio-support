Wallpaper pairs: two screens, one picture

"Wallpaper pairs" can mean a few things: couples' sets, a Lock Screen and Home Screen combo, or two images built for a phone that folds. This page is about the last one. On the foldable iPhone, the outside screen is taller than it is wide and the inside screen is wider than it is tall, so the same photo almost never works on both. A true pair fixes that by giving each screen its own crop of one picture.

Two images vs. a true pair

You can grab any two wallpapers that share a color and call it a set. It works, sort of. The difference shows the moment you open the phone: with a true pair, the picture on the cover is a close-up of the scene you see inside, so opening the phone feels like stepping back to see the whole view. That's how every set in Foldable Wallpaper Studio is built: both halves are cut from the same original artwork, at the exact pixel sizes of each screen.

Picking a pair that fits your day

There are 300+ original pairs across 14 styles, so it helps to start with a mood rather than scroll forever:

  • Work week: a quiet night sky, calm minimal textures or a solid color stay out of the way of your app icons.
  • Weekend: cute clay and 3D scenes, macaron pastels or flowers and blossoms.
  • Deep black on OLED: the pure black glow sets.
  • Something to stare at: Japanese-style illustrated scenery, lantern lanes, fantasy realms with floating islands, auroras, or classic oil painting, woodblock print and watercolor styles.
  • Seasons and weather: golden autumn, snowy scenes, rainy windows and city lights at night.

Not quite right? Every set can be recolored with the color swatches, and you can add one short line of your own text. Both the closed and open previews redraw as you change them, so you see the pair on both screens before anything is saved.

Turning your own photo into a pair

A travel photo makes a great pair: the close-up on the cover, the full landscape inside. In the Create tab, pick a photo and drag to put the focus where you want it. A few things make the result better:

  1. Pick a photo with room around the subject. The inside screen shows the wide view, so a photo cropped tight to a face or object leaves nothing to reveal when you open the phone.
  2. Put the focus on one clear thing. A person, a doorway or a single tree reads well as a close-up on the cover screen; a busy crowd doesn't.
  3. Check both previews before you save. If the cover looks crowded, nudge the focus and look again.

The app uses the system photo picker, so it only sees the photo you choose, never your whole library, and your photos stay on your device.

Make the rest of the screen match

A pair looks best when your widgets don't fight it. The app has five widgets drawn with the wallpaper you choose: My Wallpaper, Art Clock, Calendar, Battery and Text Card, in Home Screen and Lock Screen sizes. Put a matching clock on the Lock Screen and a calendar or text card on the Home Screen, and the whole phone reads as one design. The Battery widget shows only this iPhone or iPad's own charge level and charging state.

Getting the pair onto your phone

Saving puts the two images in Photos, using add-only access. iOS doesn't let apps change your wallpaper automatically, so the Settings tab in the app walks you through setting each screen; it takes a few steps. The iOS app runs on iPhone and iPad with iOS 17 or later, and the closed and open previews work on any of them.

Without a purchase you can try every tool, including HD export, recoloring, text, photo pairs and the basic widget styles, with 5 sets ready to use; images saved from those sets and from your own photos carry a small watermark. A one-time in-app purchase opens the remaining sets and removes the watermark. No ads, no account, no tracking.
